## 5. Customer personas

### Persona 1 — Farm to Freezer Custom Processing
Families across the St. Croix Valley who want an animal — theirs, or one The
SmokeHouse sources for them — turned into a freezer full of exactly the cuts they
asked for. Many split an order with family or neighbors.

- **Goals:** Animal processed safely and correctly, cut exactly to request. Everyone
  on a split order informed without becoming the go-between. Trust that what comes
  back is high quality, clearly labeled, freezer-safe for months.
- **Pain points:** Cuts coming back wrong. Vague packaging, no label, freezer burn.
  Managing a split order across people who each need their own cut list. Uncertainty
  about scheduling a slaughter date that fits their timeline.
- **Beliefs:** *"A good processor tells me what's happening before I have to ask."*
  *"I can see through the package. I know exactly what's in my freezer, and I know
  it's local."*
- **Decision motive:** Confidence the animal is handled like a real investment,
  communicated, and returned in exactly the cuts requested. For split orders,
  confidence that every person on the list gets their own call and their own cut
  list — no relay required.
- **How to win them:** Easy scheduling — phone or online, whether bringing an animal
  or needing one sourced. Call each person on a delegation list individually with
  their own cut list. Call when the order's ready; don't make them check in.

### Persona 2 — The Neighborhood Retail Customer
Rural and small-town Wisconsin households within driving distance of Durand or
Woodville who buy meat the way their grandparents did.

- **Goals:** A reliable source of quality meat week to week. Products that taste the
  way they're supposed to. Knowing where their food came from and supporting a
  business that's been part of the community a long time.
- **Pain points:** Inconsistency — good one visit, off the next. Limited selection at
  big-box stores. Feeling like a transaction, not a regular.
- **Beliefs:** *"I want to know who made this and where it came from."* *"Local means
  something to me. I'd rather pay a little more and get it right."* *"The people at
  the counter know me. That's worth showing up for."*
- **Decision motive:** Familiarity, trust, consistency.
- **How to win them:** Same quality, same product, same friendly face. Make it easy
  to know what's in stock. Let them taste something new. Recognize regulars by name.

### Persona 3 — The Wholesale Partner
Restaurants, bars, gas stations, and schools within ~30 miles of Durand and
Woodville who buy meat regularly.

- **Goals:** A local supplier who can quote pricing fast and hold to it. Repeatable
  ordering, ideally online. Delivery instead of pickup. Tell customers or students
  their food comes from a local source.
- **Pain points:** Slow or unclear pricing when margins are tight. No delivery option.
  No online ordering — everything runs through a call or a visit. Uncertainty about
  whether a local processor can handle their volume consistently.
- **Beliefs:** *"Ordering shouldn't require me to remember to call someone during
  business hours."*
- **Decision motive:** Speed and reliability of the business relationship. Rational
  and margin-driven more than emotional — **the local, real-people story is what
  breaks the tie against a distributor.**
- **How to win them:** Fast pricing — a published rate sheet or same-day quote.
  Online ordering for repeat orders. Delivery, even a limited route. Lead with the
  local story for schools and restaurants specifically.

## 6. The 7 key brand messages

**What the customer wants** — Local meat, cut right, care that's worth the trip and
not mass-produced.

**External problem** — Grocery store meat that looks the part but doesn't deliver.
Local suppliers who can't quote pricing fast, communicate clearly, or deliver
reliably. No easy way to order online. No relationship, just a transaction.

**Internal problem** — They feel unsure whether "local" is real or just a label.
Frustrated managing a split order across family members with no clear point of
contact. Anxious that their business account isn't getting straight answers on
pricing, timelines, or delivery.

**Philosophical problem** — It's wrong that meat, especially meat raised locally,
costs what it costs right now. Real care, craft, and communication shouldn't be rare,
and they shouldn't be a luxury either. They should be the standard, whether you're
feeding a family or stocking a restaurant kitchen.

**Empathy — VERBATIM**

> We know how frustrating it is to not trust what meat is in your freezer or your
> grocery cart. You deserve food that's local and cut by a team who cares.

**Authority — VERBATIM**

> The SmokeHouse has been earning this craft for decades. We source, process, and
> smoke meat the way it's supposed to be done, not the way a plant does it. Every
> order gets the same care, whether it's a split beef order or a standing wholesale
> account.

**The plan (3 steps) — VERBATIM**

1. Reach out to schedule a slaughter date or place your order, by phone or in person.
2. Get clear pricing upfront, no waiting, no guesswork.
3. Pick up your meat cut exactly to your request, labeled clearly, ready for the freezer.

**Success** — A freezer full of exactly the cuts they asked for, labeled clearly and
ready to cook. Meat they trust, from a place they actually know. A pricing
conversation that's fast, clear, and doesn't waste their time. A supplier
relationship that holds up order after order, season after season. Proof, on their
plate or their menu, that local actually means something.

**Failure** — Settling for mass-produced. A supplier who's slow to quote, hard to
reach, or disappears after the sale. Managing a split order alone, chasing down
everyone's cut list themselves.

**Identity transformation** — **From:** settling for meat they can't fully trust, and
a supplier relationship that's slow, vague, or nonexistent. **To:** a customer with a
source they trust completely, a freezer or a menu backed by real craft, and a meat
supplier who shows up every time.

## 9. Key differentiators (competitor analysis)

- **Real Dry-Aging** — Beef hangs and rests up to 30 days before it ever reaches a
  cutting board. That time builds flavor and tenderness most competitors skip
  entirely because it costs time and space they don't have.
- **Custom-to-Order** — Every order is cut to the customer's exact specs, not pulled
  from a pre-set package or a standard cut list. What you order is what you get, not
  what's convenient to produce.
- **Decades of Craft, One Crew** — Dry-aging, hand-cutting, and smoking every order
  ourselves. The SmokeHouse sources, processes, and smokes meat the way it's supposed
  to be done. *(The guide's heading reads "Decade of Craft, One Crew" while its body
  says "Decades" — treated as a typo in the guide; see `docs/open-questions.md`.)*
- **Family-Run Since Day One** — No corporate ownership, no private equity behind the
  counter. Every decision, every order, every conversation happens with the people
  who built The SmokeHouse.

**Named competitors:** JM Watkins, RJ's Meats, Pierce County Meats, A Butchery Shoppe,
Sailer's.

## 10. Voice

Plain, direct, unfussy. Short sentences. Concrete nouns — cut sheet, freezer, case,
30 days. No corporate hedging, no foodie-magazine adjectives, no cowboy cosplay. The
craft speaks; the copy doesn't oversell it. Write it the way the crew would say it
across the counter.

**Avoid:** "artisanal," "curated," "elevated," "passion for quality," exclamation
marks stacked on exclamation marks, and any tagline until one is approved.

## 12. Marketing roadmap the site should support

- **Lead magnet:** *"What to Expect When You Schedule a Custom Order"* — one-page
  guide covering slaughter date scheduling, how cut sheets work, and how split orders
  are handled. Includes a sample cut sheet. Gated by email; triggers a 4-email
  nurture sequence.
- **Weekly "in the case" post/email** — one photo, one or two lines, same format every
  time. Natural home for *See what's in the case this week*.
- **Reviews near the CTA**, not buried on a testimonials page. Respond to every Google
  review within 48 hours.
- **4-email nurture sequence:** (1) here's your guide, (2) why local actually matters,
  (3) what to expect when you schedule, (4) ready to schedule your order?
- **Ads:** Google Search on local intent keywords; geofencing around competitor
  locations.
