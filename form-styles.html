Four ways to frame the form.

All four use the real Slaughter Date Request, so what you see is what the client gets. They load as you scroll.

The honest constraint first. A JotForm embed is a third-party iframe. Nothing in our stylesheet can reach inside it, so its white ground, its blue headings and its button are fixed from our side. Everything below is about the frame we build around it. The inside is a JotForm setting, and the single biggest improvement is Jonathan changing the form’s own theme in JotForm to the shop’s cream and oxblood, and deleting the logo banner and the title from the top of the form, since the page already says both. Ten minutes of his time beats anything I can do out here.

What to send Jonathan

Three settings inside JotForm.

  1. Delete the logo banner and the form title. The page already carries the logo in the header and the form’s name on the black bar. Right now it says “Slaughter Date Request” twice within 40 pixels.
  2. Set the form theme to the shop’s colours. Background #f2ece0, buttons and required marks #7a1f26, text #171310. JotForm’s theme editor does all three.
  3. Set the form font to Archivo. It is the site’s body face and it is free in JotForm’s font list, so the embed stops reading as someone else’s page.

With those three done, any of the four options above look like part of the site. Without them, Option B is the most forgiving, because the form has room and nothing sits next to it to compare against.
